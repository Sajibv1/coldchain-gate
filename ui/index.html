Case 1 — pharmacy cold chain to the inpatient floor

Two records of one delivery, and the line between them

A floor nurse's indent arrives as an HL7 v2 order, is checked against the prescription in the FHIR record, and leaves as a notification on a phone. The left panel is what the system knows. The right panel is what the device is told. Everything the alert may contain is named in the middle column, because the boundary is an allowlist — not a filter that removes what someone remembered to remove.

What crosses

The alert is built from these four fields and nothing else. A field added upstream cannot leak, because there is no path that carries it.

  • item_description insulin glargine 100 UNT/ML Pen Injector
  • quantity 1 pen
  • courier_display_name M. OKAFOR
  • eta 14:30

The courier's name crosses because it is workforce identity, not patient data. The order numbers do not, because an identifier is linkable back to the patient — the device gets a keyed token instead.

On the nurse's phone

Everything the device receives. Four fields, and a token it cannot reverse.

Absent, not redacted: no patient name, MRN, date of birth, ward bed, or order number. The payload's keys are asserted to equal the allowlist, so a field cannot be added to the alert without failing a test.

All seven fixtures, and the verdict each one gets. Every row below is filled in from a real run, not typed: press the buttons above and watch which row changes. The trail one delivery leaves behind

The events the indent above actually wrote. Each carries its own content digest plus the digest of the event before it. Editing an event breaks its own digest; deleting one breaks the link from its successor; reordering two breaks both, because the sequence number is inside the digest. That is tamper-evident, not tamper-proof: a writer who deletes the whole chain leaves nothing to check, which is why a real deployment anchors the head digest outside the FHIR store. A rejected indent writes fewer events than a dispensed one, and the digests change between runs because the moment of recording is inside them.

What the service actually returned for this indent 
—

Every value on this page comes from app/pipeline.py — the digest, the reason codes, the four fields in the middle column. Each event's recorded timestamp is inside its digest, because a timestamp is part of what an audit record attests to, so a re-run produces different digests and that is the design rather than a caveat. The digests are produced by app/audit/chain.py over the canonical JSON of each event and checked by verify_chain(), which reads them back off the server. Run make test for the offline suite, or make demo to drive every fixture through the pipeline from a shell. The recorded runs this page falls back to are regenerated by scripts/build_ui_demo.py, and a test fails if they stop matching a real run.
